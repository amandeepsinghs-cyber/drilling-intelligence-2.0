Operational Performance Synthesis, Predictive Geopressure Control & Commercial Discovery Appraisal

Exploration wildcat well MN-SM-DW-01 was drilled by Oil and Natural Gas Corporation Limited in deepwater concession Block MN-DW-2026/01 from the dynamically positioned Class-3 drillship DS-1 in a water depth of 1,620.0 meters within the Mahanadi Offshore Basin. The primary exploration mandate was to assess the structural and stratigraphic entrapment potential of deepwater turbidite channel fairways within the Upper Miocene depositional fairway. The critical 12-1/4 inch hole section penetrated from the 13-3/8 inch intermediate casing shoe at 3,850.0 meters measured depth to a certified section Total Depth of 4,450.0 meters measured depth (4,448.2 meters true vertical depth), successfully fulfilling all subsurface evaluation objectives while maintaining a near-vertical trajectory with maximum inclination restricted to 1.40 degrees.

Flawless operational execution and zero non-productive time were achieved across eight continuous 12-hour shifts between 20 September 2026 and 24 September 2026. Total drilling time on bottom amounted to 48.0 rotating hours without a single equipment breakdown, borehole kick, or lost circulation incident, delivering the section 4.5 days ahead of statutory authorization for expenditure projections. Operational safety integrity was highlighted by zero Lost Time Incidents, zero environmental spills, and strict preservation of primary hydrostatic containment throughout the 600.0-meter open-hole trajectory.

The definitive technical milestone of the campaign occurred at 4,172.0 meters measured depth within the Unit U2 transition shale. High-frequency downhole telemetry and the Sagar Drishti agentic intelligence system provided pre-emptive detection of severe compaction disequilibrium, marked by compressional acoustic slowness surging to 101.0 microseconds per foot, corrected d-exponent inverting from 1.50 to 1.18, and connection gas spiking to 1.1%. Following formal Management of Change protocol MEMO-SM-2026-09, drilling supervisors executed an operational pause, conducted a 15-minute negative flow check, and sequentially weighted the synthetic oil-based mud system with 39.8 metric tonnes of barite from 11.20 to 11.65 pounds per gallon. This pre-emptive intervention established a 120 pounds per square inch overbalance before penetrating the reservoir at 4,195.0 meters. Upon encountering an instantaneous drilling break to 34 meters per hour at 4,205.0 meters, driller controls immediately capped penetration at 12 meters per hour, restricting dynamic equivalent circulating density to 11.86 pounds per gallon and safeguarding the 12.10 pounds per gallon shoe integrity margin.

Formation evaluation confirmed an exceptional commercial dry natural gas discovery within the Unit U3 turbidite channel complex between 4,195.0 and 4,242.0 meters measured depth. Petrophysical analyses establish 41.2 meters of net gas pay possessing an average effective porosity of 24.8%, water saturation of 26.5%, and permeability of 285 millidarcies, establishing an outstanding net-to-gross ratio of 0.88. Wireline modular formation dynamics testing verified a continuous gas fluid gradient of 0.088 pounds per square inch per foot and identified a definitive gas-water contact at 4,236.2 meters measured depth. Mud gas chromatography confirmed a dry hydrocarbon signature dominated by 88.4% methane. The Directorate recommends immediate mobilization and cementing of the 9-5/8 inch production casing string to Total Depth, followed by cased-hole drillstem flow testing to establish field commerciality for statutory filing with the Directorate General of Hydrocarbons.

Chapter 2 — Regional Geological Prognosis & Stratigraphic Succession

The Mahanadi Offshore Basin represents a high-potential polycyclic rift-to-drift passive continental margin basin along the east coast of India. Tectonostratigraphic evolution commenced during Early Cretaceous Gondwanaland rifting between India and Antarctica, establishing northeast-southwest trending basement grabens and horst blocks. Subsequent thermal subsidence during Late Cretaceous and Tertiary epochs led to widespread marine transgression, accompanied by catastrophic sediment influx from the proto-Mahanadi and Brahmani drainage networks that deposited extensive progradational clastic wedges across the continental slope.

The target stratigraphic section evaluated in the 12-1/4 inch hole consists of a complete Late Miocene to Early Pliocene bathyal marine succession. Depositional facies are characterized by deepwater hemipelagic claystones interspersed with sinuous channel-levee turbidite complexes and frontal splay sand sheets. Regional seismic mapping identified four principal lithostratigraphic intervals across the 12-1/4 inch borehole:

Unit U1 (3,850.0 m to 4,120.0 m MD): Late Miocene distal hemipelagic claystones and dark greenish-gray marine shales. The formation exhibits uniform lithological properties, high clay content (smectite-illite mixed layers), and steady normal acoustic compaction trends with compressional slowness ranging between 78 and 82 microseconds per foot.

Unit U2 (4,120.0 m to 4,195.0 m MD): Transgressive regional marine mudstones acting as the primary top and lateral capillary seal for the underlying reservoir sand. Undercompaction disequilibrium occurs prominently below 4,150.0 meters, creating a severe overpressure transition zone where pore pressure ramps rapidly from 10.95 to 11.48 pounds per gallon equivalent.

Unit U3 (4,195.0 m to 4,242.0 m MD): The primary exploration target, consisting of an Upper Miocene deepwater turbidite channel-levee sandstone complex. The reservoir exhibits excellent sorting, sub-angular to sub-rounded fine to medium quartz grains, exceptionally high net-to-gross ratio (0.88), and outstanding reservoir storage and flow capacity.

Unit U4 (4,242.0 m to 4,450.0 m MD TD): Underlying Middle to Late Miocene basal bathyal claystones, dark gray silty shales, and thin calcareous siltstone stringers, exhibiting normal hydrostatic compaction trends below the gas reservoir.

Chapter 6 — Geomechanics, Eaton Pore Pressure Inversion & 4,172 m Decision Gate

The Mahanadi Deepwater continental slope exhibits complex geomechanical stress distributions governed by high sedimentation rates and low-permeability transgressive mudstone seals. In normal compaction regimes, pore fluids expel freely, maintaining hydrostatic pore pressure gradients of 8.60 pounds per gallon equivalent (0.447 psi/ft). However, rapid sedimentation of Miocene clays generated severe compaction disequilibrium within Unit U2, trapping pore fluid and supporting a substantial portion of the overburden lithostatic load.

Real-time geomechanical pore pressure prediction was performed using Eaton's sonic slowness methodology, referenced against an empirically calibrated Normal Compaction Trend (NCT) derived from regional offset wells:

Eaton Sonic Slowness Pore Pressure Formulation

PP = σv - (σv - Phyd) × (DTnct / DTobs)3.0

Where σv is the total vertical overburden stress (calculated at 16.52 ppg equivalent based on sea water and formation bulk density integration), Phyd is normal hydrostatic pore pressure (8.60 ppg equivalent), DTnct is normal compaction slowness (78.0 μs/ft at 4,172 m), and DTobs is observed compressional acoustic slowness (101.0 μs/ft at 4,172 m).

At 4,172.0 meters measured depth, real-time telemetry detected an acute divergence in acoustic slowness, surging from 78.0 to 101.0 μs/ft within a 12-meter interval. Concurrently, the corrected d-exponent inverted from 1.50 to 1.18, and connection gas spiked to 1.1%. Inverting Eaton's equation yielded an escalating pore pressure of 11.48 pounds per gallon equivalent at the prognosed reservoir top. Because the active mud weight was 11.20 ppg, continuing to drill would have resulted in an underbalanced condition (-0.28 ppg / -155 psi) and an immediate deepwater well control kick upon penetrating the sand.

Chapter 7 — Synthetic Oil-Based Drilling Fluids & Barite Hydraulics

A low-toxicity synthetic paraffin-based invert emulsion drilling fluid system (SOBM) was utilized to drill the 12-1/4 inch hole section. The synthetic continuous phase was engineered to eliminate shale hydration and swelling in water-sensitive smectite-illite clays, deliver superior borehole lubricity, and provide strict filtration control across high-permeability reservoir sands under deepwater hydrostatic pressures.

The active surface and wellbore fluid volume totaled 1,450 barrels. Following detection of the geopressure ramp at 4,172.0 meters, drilling operations executed a controlled barite weight-up to raise mud density from 11.20 to 11.65 pounds per gallon. Rig chemical engineers executed the stoichiometric mass-balance calculation:

Barite Mass-Balance Weight-Up Equation

Wbarite = [V1 × 350 × (ρ2 - ρ1)] / (35 - ρ2)

Wbarite = [1,450 × 350 × (11.65 - 11.20)] / (35 - 11.65) = 9,780 sacks ≈ 39.8 Metric Tonnes

Where V1 is initial active mud volume (1,450 bbl), ρ1 is starting mud density (11.20 ppg), ρ2 is target mud density (11.65 ppg), and 35.0 ppg is the practical specific density constant of API drilling-grade barium sulfate (barite, BaSO4).

Chapter 10 — Autonomous AI Telemetry, Engineering Directives & Field Recommendations

Autonomous geopressure surveillance during the 12-1/4 inch section was facilitated by the Sagar Drishti agentic telemetry system, linking rig sensor data directly to the onshore Real-Time Operations Centre in Kakinada. The distributed agent network ingested 1-Hz surface drilling parameters, 5-second LWD mud-pulse telemetry, and 10-second mud gas chromatography streams, continuously comparing real-time trends against regional offset geomechanical models.

Operating under strict Human-in-the-Loop governance, the AI system functioned exclusively in an advisory capacity. At 4,172.0 meters, when Bayesian Monte Carlo simulation recommended mud weight distributions of P10 equal to 11.58 pounds per gallon, P50 equal to 11.65 pounds per gallon, and P90 equal to 11.76 pounds per gallon, the advisory was transmitted as an operational recommendation. The licensed Deepwater Drilling Superintendent retained sole executive authority, formally validating downhole indicators and issuing Management of Change order MEMO-SM-2026-09 before any physical mud adjustments commenced.

Based on the validated operational success of well MN-SM-DW-01, the following five engineering directives are formally mandated for all subsequent exploration and development drilling in the Mahanadi Deepwater Asset:

1. Mandatory Pre-emptive Mud Weight Escalation Above Geopressured Sands: Future well programs must mandate that full mud weight escalation be completed at least 20 meters prior to penetrating seismic or prognosed reservoir sand boundaries whenever entering undercompacted seal shales (formalized under Standard Operating Procedure ONGC-SM-SOP-04).

2. Automated Rate of Penetration Governance in Miocene Sands: Driller control consoles must be programmed to automatically restrict instantaneous penetration to 12.0 meters per hour whenever instantaneous penetration exceeds 25.0 meters per hour in channel sand facies, eliminating annular cuttings loading and protecting casing shoe integrity.

3. Dual-Indicator Geomechanical Pore Pressure Verification: Pore pressure escalation must be confirmed by simultaneous divergence in both compressional acoustic slowness (DT excursion greater than or equal to 8.0 microseconds per foot) and corrected d-exponent inversion, preventing unverified operational hold orders based on single-sensor telemetry.

4. Hydraulic Bottoms-Up Lag Compensation for Mud Logging: Chromatographic gas readings must be continuously corrected for annular bottoms-up circulation lag (measured at 45 minutes on drillship DS-1) to ensure accurate spatial correlation of connection gas anomalies with physical lithological boundaries.

5. Institutionalization of AI Telemetry Surveillance Under Human Governance: High-frequency autonomous multi-agent surveillance systems should be permanently integrated into the onshore Real-Time Operations Centre to monitor multi-sensor trends while retaining licensed offshore superintendents as the sole executing authorities for all management-of-change orders.
