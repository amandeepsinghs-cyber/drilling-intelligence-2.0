### 1.1 Operational Overview & Headline Achievements

Exploration well **MN-SM-DW-01** was drilled in the deepwater Mahanadi Basin in 1,620 m of water using the dynamically positioned ultra-deepwater drillship *DS-1 (illustrative)*. This report documents in technical detail the execution of the critical **12-1/4 in hole section**, spanning 3,850 m MD to total depth at 4,450 m MD. 

The primary target was the **Unit U3 Miocene turbidite slope-channel sandstone**, a proven high-productivity gas reservoir in the basin characterized by severe geopressure ramping and an exceptionally narrow operational mud weight window (0.62 ppg). Regional offset drilling had repeatedly highlighted extreme operational vulnerability in this interval: offset well **MN-DW-02** took an underbalanced gas kick of 12 bbl at 4,195 m requiring 36.5 hours of non-productive well-control operations, while offset well **MN-DW-03** experienced massive mud losses of 310 bbl at 4,222 m when an unmanaged drilling break elevated equivalent circulating density (ECD) above the formation fracture limit.

Through coordinated real-time collaboration between rig-site personnel, the onshore Real-Time Operations Centre (RTOC), and telemetry feeds integrated into the **Sagar Drishti AI System**, well MN-SM-DW-01 safely penetrated the entire target interval without a single well-control incident or formation loss. The section was completed in exactly 8 operational tours (96 operating hours) between 20 September 2026 and 24 September 2026, delivering **0.0 hours of Non-Productive Time (NPT)**.

### 1.4 Five Headline Engineering Recommendations

Based on the empirical findings and validated telemetry from the MN-SM-DW-01 campaign, the following five technical protocols are mandated for all subsequent wells in the Mahanadi Offshore Asset:

1. **Mandate Pre-emptive Mud Weight Escalation ≥ 20 m Above Pay:** Formalize ONGC-SM-SOP-04 requiring full mud weight weighting to be completed at least 20 m prior to the prognosed or seismically detected reservoir boundary whenever entering undercompacted transition shales.
2. **Automate Hard ROP Caps Upon Lithology Transition Breaks:** Mandate that driller control consoles be set to instantly restrict ROP to ≤ 12 m/hr upon encountering any rate increase exceeding 25 m/hr in Miocene sands, preventing annular cuttings loading from inflating ECD.
3. **Enforce Dual-Indicator Pore Pressure Diagnostics:** Prohibit reliance on single-sensor pore pressure estimates. Pore pressure escalation must be confirmed by simultaneous divergence in both compressional acoustic slowness (DT excursion ≥ 8 µs/ft) and the corrected d-exponent (dxc trend reversal).
4. **Incorporate Real-Time Bottoms-Up Lag Compensation:** Require mud-logging chromatographic data to be explicitly corrected for hydraulic travel time (measured at 45 minutes on DS-1) to avoid confusing lagged connection gas with fresh formation gas influx.
5. **Standardize AI Telemetry Integration with Human Authorization:** Institutionalize multi-agent advisory systems (such as Sagar Drishti) to monitor high-frequency rig telemetry while maintaining strict Human-in-the-Loop governance where licensed Drilling Superintendents execute all Management of Change orders.

### 3.1 Regional Tectonostratigraphic Framework

The Mahanadi Deepwater Basin forms a critical sector of the eastern passive continental margin of India, created during the Early Cretaceous breakup of eastern Gondwanaland. The post-rift stratigraphic architecture comprises thick progradational deltaic wedges transitioning seaward into extensive deepwater slope channels, fan lobes, and abyssal plain turbidite complexes.

In the offshore sector of MN-SM-DW-01, the post-Eocene section is dominated by high-volume terrigenous input from the proto-Mahanadi and proto-Brahmani drainage networks. Deepwater sedimentation during the Late Miocene resulted in extensive channel-levee complexes incising into semi-consolidated prodelta shales. These sandy channels—classified as **Unit U3** in this well—form outstanding hydrocarbon reservoirs enclosed above and below by thick, impermeable transgressive marine shales.

### 3.3 Detailed Stratigraphy & Lithological Descriptions (Units U1–U4)

#### Unit U1 (4,000.0 m to 4,150.0 m MD): Regional Caprock & Seal Shale
- **Lithology:** Uniform, dark grey to olive-black, moderately hard, fissile, micro-micaceous marine claystone and silty shale. Contains trace pyrite nodules and abundant planktonic foraminifera.
- **Log Signature:** High, uniform Gamma Ray (GR 95–115 API); consistent deep resistivity (Rdeep 2.2–3.5 Ω·m); bulk density averaging 2.38–2.42 g/cm³.
- **Geomechanical Behavior:** Represents normally compacted, highly competent hydrostatic shale resting firmly on the regional Normal Compaction Trend (NCT). Compressive sonic slowness remains steady at 88–90 µs/ft. Pore pressure is hydrostatic at 10.95 ppg (1.31 SG).

#### Unit U2 (4,150.0 m to 4,195.0 m MD): Geopressured Transition Zone
- **Lithology:** Interbedded light-to-medium grey calcareous siltstone, dark brownish-grey undercompacted prodelta shale, and thin, dense limestone/dolomitic stringers (0.2–0.8 m thick).
- **Log Signature:** Highly serrated GR (65–95 API); deep resistivity suppresses below the NCT; sonic compressional slowness exhibits an immediate acoustic excursion, rising rapidly from 88 µs/ft at 4,150 m to **101 µs/ft at 4,172 m**, and reaching 108 µs/ft near the base.
- **Geomechanical Significance:** Acts as the critical pressure-retention membrane isolating hydrostatic overburden from the overpressured turbidite channel beneath. Rapid pore pressure escalation initiates at 4,150 m MD.

#### Unit U3 (4,195.0 m to 4,290.0 m MD): Main Turbidite Pay Sandstone
- **Lithology:** Translucent to light grey, fine to medium-grained, moderately sorted, sub-angular to sub-rounded quartzose sandstone. Highly friable, weakly consolidated with localized calcite pore-lining cement. Framework mineralogy comprises 82% monocrystalline quartz, 11% feldspar, and 7% lithic fragments.
- **Reservoir Petrophysics:** Clean blocky log signature with exceptionally low Gamma Ray (GR 38–52 API). Deep resistivity in the gas leg ranges from **28 to 45 Ω·m**, dropping abruptly to 1.8–2.4 Ω·m across the **Gas-Water Contact at 4,270 m MD**. Core and LWD analysis demonstrate an average effective porosity (PHIE) of **24.2%**, net-to-gross ratio of **0.84**, and horizontal air permeability of **180 mD**.
- **Hydrocarbon Column:** Continuous gross dry gas column of **75.0 m** (4,195.0 m to 4,270.0 m MD). Chromatographic gas sampling confirms dry gas composition with methane (C1) exceeding 88.0% of total hydrocarbons.

#### Unit U4 (4,290.0 m to 4,460.0 m MD): Basal Miocene / Oligocene Carbonate Mudstone
- **Lithology:** Light grey to cream, dense, microcrystalline argillaceous limestone interbedded with hard, calcareous dolomitic mudstone. Highly consolidated, exhibiting low primary porosity (< 6%).
- **Log Signature:** Moderate GR (45–60 API); high deep resistivity (8–18 Ω·m); elevated sonic velocity (DT 68–74 µs/ft); bulk density 2.58–2.65 g/cm³.
- **Drilling Response:** Drilling ROP drops abruptly from 22 m/hr to 10–14 m/hr while rotary torque increases from 16 to 22 kft-lbs. Section TD was declared at 4,450 m MD within this stable, non-reservoir formation to establish a competent casing seat for the planned 9-5/8 in production string.

### 3.5 Geopressure Warning Signatures in Transition Zone U2

Overpressure in the Mahanadi Deepwater Basin is predominantly generated by **disequilibrium compaction (undercompaction)** occurring in thick, rapidly deposited Miocene prodelta clay sequences. Rapid sediment loading prevented interstitial pore fluids from expelling naturally, causing trapped pore fluids to support an increasing proportion of the overburden stress.

1. **Acoustic Slowness Excursion:** In normally compacted shale (Unit U1), compressional transit time followed a predictable logarithmic decline trendline ($DT_{NCT} = 88	ext{ }\mu	ext{s/ft}$). Beginning at 4,150 m MD, measured DT departed abruptly to the right of the NCT, escalating to **101 µs/ft at 4,172 m** (an excursion of +13 µs/ft, far exceeding the 8 µs/ft trigger threshold mandated by ONGC-SM-SOP-04).
2. **Corrected d-Exponent (dxc) Trend Reversal:** Below 4,160 m MD, the mechanical dxc index, which normally increases progressively with depth in compacting shale, displayed a definitive reverse deflection from 1.48 down to 1.18, confirming that the drill bit was penetrating an undercompacted, overpressured matrix.
3. **Connection Gas Spikes:** Contemporaneous gas monitoring revealed connection gas increasing from baseline levels of **0.4% to 1.1%** at 4,172 m, confirming proximity to an active hydrocarbon-charged geopressured reservoir.

### 5.1 Geomechanical Model & Normal Compaction Trend (NCT) Methodology

Accurate pore pressure determination in the deepwater Mahanadi Basin requires calibrating effective vertical stress against acoustic and mechanical compaction models. The geomechanical baseline for MN-SM-DW-01 was established using the **Normal Compaction Trend (NCT)** method calibrated from regional reference well MN-DW-01.

In normally compacted marine argillaceous sediments, burial and overburden expulsion cause porosity to decline exponentially with depth, accompanied by a predictable reduction in acoustic transit time:

$$\Delta t_{NCT} = \Delta t_{matrix} + (\Delta t_{mudline} - \Delta t_{matrix}) \cdot e^{-c \cdot z}$$

Where:
- $\Delta t_{matrix} = 47.6	ext{ }\mu	ext{s/ft}$ (compressive slowness of quartz/illite matrix)
- $\Delta t_{mudline} = 195.0	ext{ }\mu	ext{s/ft}$ (slowness of seawater-saturated mudline ooze)
- $c = 0.000385	ext{ m}^{-1}$ (basin compaction coefficient)
- $z = 	ext{depth below mudline (m)}$

At the base of the hydrostatic shale sequence (4,150 m MD / 2,505 m below seabed), the projected normal compaction transit time established a baseline of **$\Delta t_{NCT} = 88.0	ext{ }\mu	ext{s/ft}$**, corresponding to normal hydrostatic pore pressure of **10.95 ppg (1.31 SG / 0.440 psi/ft)**.

### 10.1 Key Operational Lessons Learned

The drilling of well MN-SM-DW-01 generated eight critical engineering lessons applicable to ultra-deepwater exploration and development campaigns:

#### Lesson 01: Mandatory Pre-emptive Mud Weight Escalation Ahead of Pay Sand Entry
- **Observation:** In deepwater turbidite environments, entering an overpressured pay sand with unweighted fluid leads to instantaneous formation influx before surface detection can occur.
- **Impact:** Offset MN-DW-02 entered Unit U3 with 11.15 ppg fluid, taking a 12 bbl kick that consumed 36.5 hours of NPT. MN-SM-DW-01 completed weighting to 11.65 ppg (1.40 SG) 23 m above the sand, ensuring a +120 psi overbalance upon entry.
- **Recommendation:** Mandate that full mud weight-up be completed at least 20 m prior to the prognosed reservoir boundary.
- **Linked Standard Operating Procedure:** **ONGC-SM-SOP-04 (Pore Pressure Ramp Weight-up Protocol)**.

#### Lesson 02: Hard ROP Caps at Lithological Drilling Breaks Protect Casing Shoe Integrity
- **Observation:** Instantaneous penetration rates in friable deepwater turbidites can exceed 35 m/hr, creating acute annular solids loading and severe ECD surges.
- **Impact:** Offset MN-DW-03 drilled uncapped at 38 m/hr, causing ECD to breach the 13-3/8 in shoe FIT and inducing 310 bbl of mud losses. On MN-SM-DW-01, capping ROP at 12.0 m/hr kept ECD at 11.86 ppg, preserving a 0.24 ppg safety margin.
- **Recommendation:** Implement automated driller console set-points restricting ROP to ≤ 12 m/hr immediately upon encountering penetration rate breaks > 25 m/hr in Miocene sands.
- **Linked Standard Operating Procedure:** **ONGC-HC-SOP-05 (Hole Cleaning and Annular Pressure Management)**.

#### Lesson 03: Dual-Signal Confirmation Overcomes Single-Sensor Geopressure Ambiguity
- **Observation:** Acoustic slowness can fluctuate locally across calcareous stringers, while mechanical d-exponent is sensitive to bit wear and hydraulic fluctuations.
- **Impact:** Relying on a single indicator introduces uncertainty that can delay executive decision-making. Requiring concurrent divergence in both acoustic slowness (DT excursion ≥ 8 µs/ft) and corrected d-exponent (dxc reversal) enabled immediate, confident authorization of MOC MEMO-SM-2026-09.
- **Recommendation:** Codify a dual-indicator requirement into deepwater drilling programmes: both acoustic and mechanical trends must diverge before triggering mud weight escalation.
- **Linked Standard Operating Procedure:** **ONGC-SM-SOP-04**.

#### Lesson 04: Rig-Specific Bottoms-Up Lag Synchronization is Essential for Hydrocarbon Interpretation
- **Observation:** In 1,620 m of water, cuttings and mud gas require 45 minutes to travel from the drill bit to the surface shakers at 850 GPM.
- **Impact:** At an ROP of 22 m/hr, surface gas indications lag the bit by 16.5 m; at 34 m/hr, lag expands to 25.5 m. Real-time LWD telemetry detected the transition zone 45 minutes prior to cuttings arrival at surface.
- **Recommendation:** Mud logging systems must dynamically compute and display the instantaneous lagged depth alongside real-time bit depth on all rig-floor and RTOC displays.
- **Linked Standard Operating Procedure:** **ONGC-WCR-SOP-09 (Standard Well Logging and Reporting Protocols)**.

#### Lesson 05: Controlled Barite Addition Rates Prevent Dynamic Rheological Shock
- **Observation:** Rapid barite additions to synthetic oil-based muds can cause emulsifier starvation, barite sag, and sudden spikes in plastic viscosity.
- **Impact:** Adding 87,700 lb (39.8 MT) of barite across exactly 3.0 hours (~1.5 circulations) allowed concurrent dosing of base oil and secondary wetting agents, keeping Plastic Viscosity constant at 28 cP and Yield Point at 18 lb/100 ft².
- **Recommendation:** Enforce a maximum barite addition rate of 15.0 MT/hr in deepwater SOBM systems, supported by continuous hourly pilot testing.
- **Linked Standard Operating Procedure:** **ONGC-MC-SOP-02 (Drilling Fluid Weighting and Emulsion Stability)**.

#### Lesson 06: Streamlined Digital MOC Workflows Minimize Operational Idle Time
- **Observation:** Traditional paper-based Management of Change procedures can stall operations for multiple hours, increasing borehole exposure risks.
- **Impact:** Digital telemetry transmission and pre-configured MOC templates enabled the joint rig/RTOC team to evaluate, endorse, and approve MEMO-SM-2026-09 within 60 minutes, with zero non-productive rig downtime.
- **Recommendation:** Formalize digital MOC workflows for all critical deepwater operations, pre-delegating approval authority to the on-duty Drilling Superintendent when supported by RTOC technical consensus.
- **Linked Standard Operating Procedure:** **ONGC-MOC-SOP-07 (Management of Change Governance)**.

#### Lesson 07: Static Flow Checks Prior to Weight-Up Guarantee Wellbore Baseline
- **Observation:** Weighting up a well that has already suffered a kick can result in gas migration into the riser while circulating.
- **Impact:** Performing a rigorous 15-minute static flow check on the trip tank at 4,172 m confirmed zero influx before starting barite additions, ensuring that circulation remained safe throughout.
- **Recommendation:** Mandate a 15-minute static flow check prior to initiating any fluid density adjustment exceeding 0.3 ppg.
- **Linked Standard Operating Procedure:** **ONGC-WC-SOP-01 (Well Control Barrier Management)**.

#### Lesson 08: Strict Human Authorization Preserves Operational Accountability
- **Observation:** Autonomous AI systems provide exceptional predictive speed but lack physical accountability and contextual legal responsibility.
- **Impact:** Operating Sagar Drishti strictly in an advisory capacity maintained clear operational command hierarchy: every rig action was deliberately approved and executed by licensed ONGC personnel, supported by an immutable cryptographic audit ledger.
- **Recommendation:** Institutionalize strict Human-in-the-Loop governance as a permanent operational standard across all AI-assisted drilling campaigns.
- **Linked Standard Operating Procedure:** **ONGC-SM-SOP-04**.
